NeoBalady+ Multi-model AI for smart-city operations

Our graduation project: an AI backend exploring how a Balady-style municipal complaints workflow could go from reactive inbox to predictive city brain — six specialized models running together on a single T4 GPU, built and evaluated as an academic prototype.

  • periodsep 2025 — jun 2026
  • rolegraduation project · team
  • headlineR² 0.963 SLA forecast

the problem

A large city receives thousands of municipal reports weekly — potholes, broken lights, infrastructure damage — and in the traditional workflow, staff classify, prioritize, and dispatch every ticket by hand. Photos are exaggerated, duplicates flood in from different angles and times of day, and nobody knows how long a repair will actually take.

The engineering constraint made it harder: everything had to run on one T4 GPU. Qwen2-VL (7B), a fine-tuned Whisper (764M), a ConvLSTM, a Random Forest, and RAG pipelines — simultaneously. That meant aggressive quantization and careful scheduling, not just model picking.

how it works

  1. Vision-language triage — Qwen2-VL-7B in 4-bit

    One citizen photo drives five tasks: complaint classification (76% accuracy), Arabic description generation, day/night-invariant duplicate detection, objective priority assessment that overrides exaggerated reports, and before/after repair verification for contractors.

  2. Saudi-dialect voice complaints — Whisper + Qwen

    Whisper-medium fine-tuned on SADA22 (420 hours of Saudi Arabic speech) transcribes voice notes; Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct cleans the raw transcript while preserving the local dialect. ALLaM was tested, Qwen won on instruction-following.

  3. SLA prediction — Random Forest

    A 300-estimator RandomForestRegressor predicts resolution time in days from category, priority, timestamp, and location — R² = 0.963, so citizens get a real date instead of a shrug.

  4. Proactive forecasting — ConvLSTM on the real street grid

    A 2M-parameter ConvLSTM predicts tomorrow's complaint distribution on a 32×32 grid built from a real city street network (via OSMnx). A custom HotspotAwareLoss (25× miss penalty) stops it from lazily predicting zero everywhere; z-score anomaly detection (3σ) fires alerts before reports exist.

  5. Two RAG assistants

    A citizen chatbot for report tracking and FAQs (Arabic, grounded to avoid hallucination) and a municipality assistant that answers staff questions about reports and KPIs in natural language.

  6. Gradio operations dashboards

    Every module ships with an interactive dashboard — urban radar heatmap, report lifecycle, KPI summaries, and the chat interfaces. Not a demo: the operating console.
