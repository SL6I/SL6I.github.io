Saudi Dialect End-of-Utterance Detection

Voice agents interrupt people because no model knows when a Saudi speaker is actually done. This one does — from text alone, fast enough for a live call.

  • periodjan 2026
  • rolesolo project
  • headline77.47 F1 · ~110M params

the problem

Reply too early and the agent interrupts the user. Reply too late and the conversation feels broken. End-of-utterance detection is the model that decides the exact moment to speak — and for Saudi dialect, with its natural pauses, hesitations, and local vernacular, off-the-shelf models simply don't solve it.

Two constraints shaped everything: no public dataset captured real conversational Saudi dialect with pause/incomplete-turn signals, and the decision must land in milliseconds inside a live call — ruling out heavy 7B+ generative LLMs from the start.

how it works

  1. A smart hybrid dataset

    Curated public Saudi-dialect data (Najdi, Hijazi, 44 domains) combined with 2,136 synthetic examples generated by ALLaM, the Saudi LLM — specifically covering hesitations and edge cases the public data missed.

  2. Preprocessing that mimics real ASR

    Text normalized to look like raw speech-to-text output: unified Alef/Ya, stripped diacritics and punctuation. Incomplete samples generated by trimming final words, then upsampled to a clean 50/50 balance.

  3. Why BERT, not an LLM

    Bidirectional context plus ~110M parameters equals real-time speed. Benchmarked three Arabic encoders: SaudiBERT (~79.32 F1) beat MARBERTv2 (~76.52) and CAMeLBERT-DA — its Saudi text corpus gave it the strongest grasp of local idioms.

  4. Fine-tune and ship

    Fine-tuned at LR 1e-5, batch 16, best-checkpoint saving — then shipped as a Python SDK plus a LiveKit adapter that intercepts the STT stream and fires a REPLY signal above a 0.75 confidence threshold. Model, dataset, and live demo are public on Hugging Face.
